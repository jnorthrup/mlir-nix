# The sea of nodes, demand-minted

Cliff Click's IR is a graph of nodes with explicit use-def edges, no fixed order, and a separate
schedule that decides when a node exists and where it runs. The `nix` dialect is already the first
half: its regions are graph regions of mostly pure ops, forced on demand. What it lacked was the
second half. Every op of a file was created when the file was lowered, and 89% of them are never
evaluated (nixos-toplevel: 121,687 of 1,095,819). This change moves one decision: a lambda body is
minted on its first call, not when its file is lowered. Value numbering is not part of it; it was
measured not to pay here (FABRIC.md; F17 in COMBINATORS.md).

## What was built, in PRELOAD's terms

`include/Composite/Series.h` is the kernel algebra in C++, and only what the schedule consumes.

| PRELOAD | here | consumer |
|---|---|---|
| `a j b` | `composite::Join`, infix `a |j| b`; `body_index` of a `nix.lambda` is `file j row`, a `TwInt` in an `i64` attribute | every deferred lambda |
| `Series`, no bounds check | `composite::Series`, `series(ptr, n)`; indices come from the builder that issued them | the free-name view in `Gen::deferRecipe` |
| `xs α f` | `xs |α| f`, lazy; the fusion law is a `static_assert` | each free name projected through the live scope into a capture |
| `MutableSeries` (accumulates, then drains) | `composite::MutableSeries` | the columns of the pool |
| `Cursor` + `ColumnMeta` | `composite::Cursor<T...>`, one `MutableSeries` per column, column names | `nixfe::FilePool`, printed by `MLNIX_STATS` under its own column names |
| `↺` anchor | the forced `body` cell, and the constants anchored at the head of a region (`Gen::baseRef`) | `EvalState::bodyOf` |

Not written, because nothing calls them: `leftIdentity` as a thunk, `RowVec`, `get(range)`, Confix.

How it works:

- Lowering a lambda in `MLNIX_DEFER` mode creates the `nix.lambda` with an empty body block, its
  **captures** as operands (the `with` chain innermost first, then the free names that resolve in
  the live scope, base-environment names included), and `body_index`. It appends one row to the
  file's pool: `(expr, op, withs, names, body)`.
- Captures are real operands. The cleanup after devirtualization erases an unused `nix.builtin`,
  `nix.select` or `nix.str`; a value that only a waiting body will read must already have a use. The
  placeholder RAUW of a `let`/`rec` group also keeps them honest.
- The free names come from one memoized walk (`FreeVars`): a body is walked once however deep the
  lambdas nest. A name that no scope can supply is an error at lowering time, exactly when the eager
  schedule reports it, unless a `with` could supply it (`x: y` must fail without being called).
- The first call drains the row (`EvalState::mintLambda`): the scope is rebuilt from the captures (a
  flat lexical map, then the `with` chain: a lexical binding beats every `with`, so this is the same
  scope), the body is generated by the same function as in the eager schedule, devirtualized, and
  indexed. The row keeps its anchor, the indexed region. Every later call reads the anchor.
- A lambda is not `IsolatedFromAbove`, so no `PassManager` can run on it. The demand schedule runs
  devirtualization directly with its pattern set frozen once, and refuses any `MLNIX_PIPELINE` other
  than `nix-prims`.

## The A/B

`scripts/son-ab.py`, modeled on TrikeShed's shape benches (`src/commonBench/kotlin/borg`): the same
work down two paths, the whole path priced, parameters swept, the answer checked inside the run (a
run that fails or answers differently stops the table). Acceptance was stated in the script before
the numbers: demand beats eager in wall time on both real workloads, with identical result hashes
and a lower peak footprint.

    arms:   eager (default)   demand (MLNIX_DEFER=1)        7 interleaved runs, medians, macOS arm64

    workload          wall eager   wall demand      footprint eager   footprint demand   hashes
    nixos-toplevel      2.782 s      2.549 s  -8.4%     1884 MB          1756 MB  -6.8%    identical
    nixpkgs-73          2.304 s      2.075 s -10.0%     1793 MB          1648 MB  -8.1%    identical

The acceptance holds. The estimate in PERFORMANCE.md (12-17% and 300+ MB) was high by about a
third on time and by half on memory. Why, from `MLNIX_STATS`:

    nixos-toplevel            eager       demand
    ops lowered             1,095,819     619,842   (-43%)
    ops ever evaluated        121,687     121,680   (11.1% -> 19.6% of those lowered)
    lowering                    879 ms       72 ms
    minted on demand              -         506 ms
    lambdas                       -         29,744 deferred, 5,394 called (82% never)

    nixpkgs-73: ops 798,177 -> 384,645, lowering 642 ms -> 104 + 294 ms, 23,376 deferred, 3,537 called

The wall time saved is the lowering saved: 879 - (72 + 506) = 301 ms against 233 ms measured on
toplevel, 244 ms against 229 ms on nixpkgs-73. 82% of lambdas are never called, but a called lambda
mints its whole body, and most of a called body's ops are still never evaluated. Lambda grain
removes 43-52% of the ops while 89-90% of the eager schedule's ops are dead.

Lowering under demand takes 72 ms on toplevel (104 ms on nixpkgs-73) in all: the analysis of every
lambda in every file read, the shells, the captures, and the top-level code that is not a lambda.
The plan's memory is about 5 MB: 94,805 free names analyzed (32 bytes each), 56,135 names pooled
(16 bytes each), and 29,744 pool rows of 36 bytes.

### Shapes: where it stops paying

Generated files of 20,000 pattern lambdas with 12-entry bodies (TrikeShed's LCG picks which are
called), 5 interleaved runs. Hashes identical on every row.

    called                wall eager   wall demand            footprint
    0%                      0.829 s      0.308 s  -62.8%      475 -> 186 MB  -60.8%
    1%                      0.826 s      0.308 s  -62.7%      478 -> 188 MB  -60.7%
    10%                     0.836 s      0.363 s  -56.6%      479 -> 210 MB  -56.3%
    50%                     0.839 s      0.607 s  -27.7%      473 -> 314 MB  -33.5%
    100%                    0.882 s      0.922 s   +4.6%      475 -> 428 MB   -9.8%
    50 lambdas x 50k calls  1.163 s      1.129 s   -2.9%     1786 -> 1785 MB   0.0%

Wall time breaks even near 93% of lambdas called (interpolated between the 50% and 100% rows; not
measured there). At 100% called the plan is pure cost: +4.6% wall. Real nixpkgs sits at 18%. The
hot row is the call path: the anchored cell is slightly cheaper than the `DenseMap` probe it
replaces, so calling a minted lambda does not regress.

## Failures and corrections

- The first `MLNIX_DEFER` lang run failed 50 of 466: `PassManager::run` refuses a root that is not
  `IsolatedFromAbove`, and a lambda reads its captures from outside. Devirtualization now runs
  directly on the minted body.
- The estimate (12-17%, 300+ MB) was too high; see above for what it missed.
- No A/B of the pool's shape (columnar `Cursor` against a struct per row) was run: 29,744 rows of 36
  bytes are 1 MB, three orders below the effect being measured. The columnar form is there because
  it is the algebra's pool, and it costs nothing measurable.

## Not built, with the number that gates it

Type pools for every node (the whole AST as `Cursor`s of typed instances, Confix mapping a tree to
rows) have a consumer only if deferral goes finer than the lambda: an attribute value, a `let`
binding, an argument, minted when it is forced. The headroom, from the table above: after lambda
grain, 80.4% of the ops that toplevel still lowers are never evaluated, and the bodies it mints cost
506 ms (294 ms on nixpkgs-73). If the never-evaluated ops cost what the rest cost, thunk grain is
worth up to about 400 ms (16% of the demand wall) on toplevel and 235 ms (11%) on nixpkgs-73, before
the price of a finer plan, which at lambda grain was 72 ms of lowering and grows with the number of
rows. A frame per deferred thunk and a region per row are the design risks. That is the next
measurement to make, not a conclusion.

The default stays eager. Flipping it needs the paths that print or re-read lowered IR
(`--dump-mlir`, `mlnix compile`, `.mlir` input) to force eager, because a deferred lambda is a shell
whose expression lives in the arena.
